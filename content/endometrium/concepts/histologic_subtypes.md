# Sous-types histologiques

Status: needs_review

## Fact

Endometrial carcinoma includes endometrioid and non-endometrioid/aggressive histologies such as serous carcinoma, clear cell carcinoma and carcinosarcoma.

## Why It Matters

Histology is a biological signal. It influences baseline risk, expected routes of spread, molecular testing priorities and adjuvant treatment discussions.

## Clinical Consequence

Aggressive histology can move a patient into a higher-risk reasoning pathway even when uterine-confined disease seems limited anatomically.

## RCP Consequence

The RCP must check:

- exact histologic type;
- grade;
- p53/MMR/POLE status when relevant;
- LVSI;
- nodal staging;
- peritoneal/omental evaluation if clinically relevant.

## Common Trap

Treating all "stage I endometrial cancers" as comparable. Histology and molecular class can radically change the risk conversation.

## Micro-Question

Why can a serous carcinoma limited to the uterus be discussed more aggressively than a low-grade endometrioid tumor?

Expected reasoning: serous histology is associated with aggressive biology and higher extrauterine/distant relapse risk.

## Prerequisites

- uterine histology
- tumor grade
- molecular classification
