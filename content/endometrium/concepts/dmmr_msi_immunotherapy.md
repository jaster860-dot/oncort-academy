# dMMR/MSI et immunothérapie

Status: needs_review

## Fact

Mismatch repair deficiency and microsatellite instability identify tumors with high immunogenicity and major implications for Lynch syndrome screening and systemic therapy.

## Why It Matters

dMMR/MSI status is both a biological clue and a treatment biomarker. It matters for family/genetic evaluation and for immunotherapy decisions, particularly in recurrent or advanced disease.

## Clinical Consequence

In localized disease, MMR status contributes to molecular classification and risk discussion. In recurrent/metastatic disease, it can guide immune checkpoint inhibitor use depending on line, setting and guideline context.

## RCP Consequence

The radio-oncologist should understand:

- why MMR IHC is requested;
- when MLH1 promoter methylation testing matters;
- why Lynch syndrome must be considered;
- how dMMR/MSI changes systemic therapy options;
- immune-related toxicities that can affect RT planning or follow-up.

## Common Trap

Seeing MMR only as a pathology checkbox. It connects diagnosis, genetics, prognosis and systemic treatment.

## Micro-Question

Why does dMMR/MSI biology make immune checkpoint inhibition biologically plausible?

Expected reasoning: deficient DNA mismatch repair increases mutational burden/neoantigens, making immune recognition more likely.

## Prerequisites

- DNA repair basics
- tumor immunology basics
- molecular classification
